# BIRD-001: accepted archived-attribute illustration

In one prespecified archived-attribute illustration using the positive-contact
presence projection of [ASNR Network 319 at its frozen commit](https://github.com/bansallab/asnr/blob/3678a65a6824e09d539b18bfb702235b3c1f78b8/Networks/Network_319.graphml)
(17 labeled birds, 53 edges), the sufficient ordinary Curveball schedule
completed 199 outputs and gave raw rank .065 plus a .005 approximation allowance,
or **.070**. The older Besag-Clifford shared-hub rank was also **.070**;
neither rejected at .05. Direct whole-pairing rejection exhausted its first
fixed one-million-candidate draw without a graph and stopped, so its p-value
was withheld. One draw failed and 198 scheduled outputs remained unstarted.
This is not a failure of exact algorithms as a class.

The selected null is uniform over all labeled simple undirected graphs with
each bird's fixed distinct-partner degree; disconnected graphs are allowed,
with no sex or spatial conditioning.

The sufficient schedule was computationally feasible on this input; its
distributional approximation remains conditional on ideal randomness and kernel
assumptions. No practical advantage, power/size calibration or original-paper
replication was demonstrated. Archived Color was used unchanged. Historical
sex standardization and observation-window identity remain unresolved, so this
does not reproduce Fosdick's published .001 result. Non-rejection does not
validate the biological null or establish causal plumage preference.

The frozen sufficient arm used 2,138,056 attempted pair trades; the significance-only
Besag-Clifford arm used 340,000. These methods assure different targets: conditional
joint distributional approximation versus an exchangeable rank test. Pipeline
costs are not a general sampler/backend speed comparison. Existing valid tests
predate this planner; additional computation buys an explicit error allowance,
not a measured improvement in scientific utility. See [the practical conclusion](CONTRIBUTION.md).
